How Pigeon sets prices

Every price you see in the Pigeon app comes from one small machine called the market maker. This note explains what it does, why prices move, and how much money Pigeon can make or lose, without assuming any maths.

The short version

  • A share pays ₦100 if its outcome happens and ₦0 if it doesn't. Its price, between ₦1 and ₦99, is the crowd's estimate of the chance.
  • Users always trade with Pigeon's market maker, an automatic dealer that is always willing to buy or sell and nudges prices with every trade.
  • The market seed an admin sets when creating a market controls how jumpy prices are, and it is also the most Pigeon can ever lose on that market.
  • Pigeon earns a 2% fee on every buy and every sell. Pigeon's market maker wins money when the crowd's favourite turns out wrong, and loses some when everyone correctly piles onto the winner.

01 What people are buying

Take the question “Who will be the next Nigerian President?” with four candidates, A to D. On Pigeon, every candidate has its own shares. A share in Candidate B is a promise: if B wins, Pigeon pays the holder ₦100 for that share. If B loses, the share is worth nothing.

So if a B share costs ₦25, the market is saying B has roughly a 25% chance. That is why the app shows the price and the “chance” as the same number.

Think of it like

A voucher that reads “Redeem for ₦100 if B wins.” Nobody would pay ₦90 for it unless they were nearly certain B wins, and nobody would sell it for ₦5 unless they thought B was hopeless. The price people pay for the voucher is their opinion, measured in naira.

02 Who is on the other side of the trade

In a normal betting shop, or on a stock exchange, a buyer needs a seller. Early on, with few users, there often isn't one, and trades would just sit there. Pigeon solves this with an automated market maker: a piece of software that is always ready to sell you shares, or buy them back, at a price it calculates on the spot.

Think of it like

A bureau de change that never closes. You can always walk up and swap money; it never says “come back when someone else wants dollars.” But if everyone rushes in to buy dollars, the rate it quotes climbs with every customer, and when people start selling dollars back, the rate eases. Pigeon's market maker does the same with shares.

03 Why prices move

The market maker follows two rules:

  1. The chances always add up to 100%. Four candidates start at 25% each. If one goes up, the others go down by the same total.
  2. Buying pushes a price up, selling pushes it down. Every share bought is a vote for that outcome, so its price rises a little.
Think of it like

A pie that is always exactly one pie. Buying into Candidate B makes B's slice bigger, and the only place that space can come from is everyone else's slices.

04 The market seed: how deep the pool is

When an admin creates a market they enter a market seed, for example ₦100,000. The seed does two jobs at once:

  1. It sets how much prices move per trade. A bigger seed means steadier prices; a smaller seed means a single large trade can swing the market.
  2. It caps Pigeon's risk. The maths guarantees Pigeon can never lose more than the seed on that market, no matter who buys what or which outcome wins. Fees collected come on top of that.
Think of it like

Throwing a stone into water. In a puddle (small seed), the splash is huge. In a lake (big seed), the same stone barely makes a ripple. The size of the lake is also what you have committed to the game: a lake costs more to fill, but it is calmer.

06 Selling before the result

Users don't have to wait for the outcome. They can sell shares back to the market maker at any time while the market is open. Selling runs the same machine in reverse: the price drops a little with each share sold, and Pigeon takes a 2% fee from the proceeds.

If B's price has risen since Ama bought, she can sell for a profit without waiting for the election. If it has fallen, she can sell to cut her losses.

Think of it like

Selling a match ticket back before kickoff. If the fixture has become the hottest game in town, you get more than you paid. If interest has faded, you get less, but you get something now instead of waiting to see the result.

07 When the result is known

  1. An admin marks the winning outcome. Trading stops immediately.
  2. There is a 10-minute settlement window in which the admin can correct a mistake.
  3. After the window, every winning share pays ₦100 into its holder's wallet, and losing shares pay nothing. Everyone involved gets a “You won” or “You lost” notification. This happens automatically once the scheduled settlement job is switched on; until then an admin triggers it.
  4. If a market has to be cancelled, for example a match is abandoned, it is voided: everyone gets back what they paid for the shares they still hold. Fees are not refunded.

08 How Pigeon makes, and can lose, money

There are two separate money flows:

FeesAlways income. 2% on every buy and every sell, win or lose.

Think of it like

The seed is a capped marketing budget for each market. Pigeon spends at most that much to make sure there is always a price to trade at, and gets fees back on all the trading it attracts. A well-traded market usually pays back its seed; a quiet, one-sided market can use it up.

Because Pigeon is on the other side of every trade, regulators are likely to treat the platform like a bookmaker. That is worth raising in licensing conversations.

09 The decisions this leaves to you

Seed per market

Bigger seeds feel more professional (prices don't lurch) but put more money at risk. Big, popular markets deserve big seeds; niche ones can start small.

Fee rate

Currently 2% on buys and sells. It can be changed platform-wide, or set per market, without an app update.

Which markets to open

Markets where opinion is genuinely split attract trading on several sides, which is where fees outweigh the seed risk.

10 Safety built in

  • No overspending. A trade only goes through if the user's wallet covers the amount plus the fee.
  • No nasty surprises. If other trades move the market enough that the user would get more than 2% less than the review screen showed, the trade is refused and re-quoted.
  • No double-counting. Trades on the same market are processed strictly one at a time, and every naira in or out of a wallet is written to a permanent ledger that cannot be edited.
  • Rounding can't create money. Amounts are rounded to the kobo, always in the direction that keeps Pigeon's books whole, so any rounding difference is a fraction of a kobo in Pigeon's favour.
  • 18+ only. Nobody can trade without a date of birth showing they are an adult.

11 For the curious: the actual maths

The formal name for this market maker is the Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule (LMSR), designed by the economist Robin Hanson and used by many prediction markets. You don't need this section to run the business; it's here so an engineer, investor or regulator can check the claims above.

Let qᵢ be the number of shares sold so far of outcome i, and b the liquidity set by the seed.

Price of outcome i pᵢ = e^(qᵢ/b) ÷ Σⱼ e^(qⱼ/b) Total cost function C(q) = b · ln( Σⱼ e^(qⱼ/b) ) Cost of any trade C(after) − C(before) Liquidity from seed b = seed ÷ (₦100 × ln n) n = number of outcomes Worst-case loss ₦100 × b × ln n = seed

For the four-candidate market with a ₦100,000 seed, b = 100,000 ÷ (100 × ln 4) ≈ 721.35 shares. The prices always sum to exactly 1, which is why the chances always add up to 100%.

12 Glossary

Share
A claim that pays ₦100 if its outcome happens and ₦0 otherwise.
Price or chance
What a share costs right now, in naira out of ₦100, which doubles as the market's estimated probability.
Market maker
Pigeon's automatic dealer: always quotes a price, takes the other side of every trade, and moves prices as people trade.
Market seed
The amount set when a market is created. It controls how steady prices are and is the maximum Pigeon can lose on that market.
Slippage
The gap between the price you see and the average price you pay on a larger trade, because your own buying pushes the price up.
Settlement
Paying ₦100 per winning share, 10 minutes after the result is entered.
Void
Cancelling a market and refunding what people paid for their shares.
